1. Temperature
2. Humidity
3. Cloudiness
4. Precipitation (rain, snow, sleet, hail)

1. Average temperature
2. Average precipitation
3. Seasonal patterns (e.g., winter, summer, monsoon)
4. Extreme weather events (e.g., hurricanes, droughts)

Climate is a more stable and predictable concept than weather. It helps us understand the overall conditions that a region experiences over an extended period.

To illustrate the difference, consider this example:

* Weather: "It's raining today in New York City."
* Climate: "New York City experiences an average of 40 inches of rainfall per year, with most of it falling during the spring and summer months."

In summary, weather is the short-term and local condition of the atmosphere, while climate is the long-term average atmospheric condition in a region.
